## What the owner asked for (2026-10-15)

Night becomes the main mood of the game, and the player has only a flashlight. Do the night and the flashlight now; the dinosaurs come later. The owner's design intent, which the flashlight should serve: the light lets you see but will later also give you away to the dinosaurs, so turning it off to hide is part of the game, and the moment a dinosaur appears between the trees should land inside the beam.

## What to build

1. A `Night` lighting preset for the forest, made the scene's default. `OvercastMist` stays buildable (menu item and a command-line flag). The night must read as night and as horror: very dark, a faint cold moonlight or sky glow so trunks and the trail near the player are barely readable without the flashlight (about 5 to 10 m), never a pure black screen, and mist that catches the flashlight's beam. With the flashlight on, the beam shows detail out to about 20 to 25 m and dies into the mist. Near foliage hit by the beam must not blow out to white.
2. A player flashlight as a runtime component, for example `Assets/Game/Scripts/Player/PlayerFlashlight.cs`: an HDRP spot light on the camera pivot with realistic output for a handheld torch (state the lumens), soft shadows, an inner and outer cone, a beam pattern from a cookie texture generated by code (no downloaded or hand-painted texture), a slight smoothed lag behind the view so it feels hand-held, a visible volumetric beam in the mist, and a toggle bound in `PlayerInputReader` (keyboard F; pick a sensible gamepad button). It starts switched on. Expose `IsOn` and an event when it changes, for the AI later. No battery yet.
3. The forest's player rig gets the flashlight. The greybox does not change in this step.
4. Performance: the budget above holds with the flashlight on and casting shadows, in the player build. Measure night with the flashlight off and on against D2's figures, and choose shadow resolution and update settings that fit.
5. Tests: add tests for the flashlight (toggles, exists on the forest player, starts on) and a night exposure sanity check (frames are dark but not black with the light off; the lit area is readable and not clipped with it on). The forest EditMode and PlayMode suites and the greybox's seven tests must pass.
6. Captures of the night into `SCRATCH/build/captures/n1-*`: the standard set with the flashlight on, and a few views with it off. Look at every image.

3. One Unity process at a time.
