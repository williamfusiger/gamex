# N1: night as the forest's main mood, and the player's torch

2026-10-15. Brief: `build/BRIEF-night.md`. `B` = `SCRATCH/build`, `P` = the project.

## 1. Result

- **Night is the scene's default** (`ForestLightingPresets.Default = "Night"`); OvercastMist stays in the scene and buildable (menu
  `Gamex/Forest/Preset/Overcast Mist`, flag `-forestPreset OvercastMist`) and renders as before (RMSE to D2's captures 0.0018 to
  0.0021). Moonless night sky 0.1 lux, moon 0.3 lux at 4,100 K, mist 30 m, automatic exposure -1.2 .. 10 EV metered on a disc in the
  middle of the screen, a +0.25 gamma on the dark tones. Without the torch: very dark, trunks black against the moon's glow in the mist,
  trail ruts, ferns and a boulder barely readable for the first metres (frame means 0.03 to 0.08, never a black frame). With it: the
  beam lights the trail, firs and logs, a shaft from the hand and a haze in the mist, a grey card readable at 20 to 25 m and fading at
  30 m; near foliage keeps its detail (at most 0.19 % of a frame clipped).
- **The torch**: `PlayerFlashlight` (runtime, `Gamex.Runtime`), 300 lm (peak 2,860 cd), cones 10 / 70 degrees, range 35 m, 4,500 K,
  soft shadows 1,024 px updated every frame, code-generated beam pattern, volumetric dimmer 0.04, smoothed hand-held lag (0.07 s, at most
  10 degrees), F / gamepad left shoulder, starts on, `IsOn` + `IsOnChanged`. On the forest's player only.
- **Player frame time** (macOS development player, 2,560 x 1,440, vsync off, 16-frame windows, worst of 29 views, all runs bursty):
  final session, D2's own final player interleaved: **D2 9.62 / 11.73 ms (median / p95), night torch off 10.18 / 11.42, night torch on
  11.51 / 12.67 ms** against 12.5 / 15. Across the day's sessions torch on 11.3 to 12.5 ms at pano_90. The torch costs +0.5 to +1.3 ms
  a view (median 0.9), its shadows +0.1 to +0.8 (median 0.6); a 512-pixel map costs the same.
- **Tests**: forest EditMode **40/40**, forest PlayMode **13/13**, greybox **7/7** (`out/N1/tests/final2/*.xml`). Triangle and
  vertex budgets re-based for the torch's shadow map (section 6). Captures `captures/n1-final` (32 views, torch on), `n1-dark`
  (10 views, torch off), `n1-overcast` (6 views), `n1-final/debugset`, play-mode stills `captures/n1-tests/final*/playmode`; every
  image looked at.

## 2. The torch (step 1)

`P/Assets/Game/Scripts/Player/PlayerFlashlight.cs` (new, `Gamex.Runtime`; the asmdef is unchanged, so the component uses only
`UnityEngine` and `UnityEngine.Rendering.LightUnit`, both in the engine):

- On the torch's own object (`Player/CameraPivot/Flashlight`), `[RequireComponent(typeof(Light))]`. `IsOn`, `event Action<bool>
  IsOnChanged` (raised once per real change), `SetOn(bool)`, `Toggle()`, `SnapToView()`. Starts on (`startOn = true`). No battery.
- Input: `PlayerInputReader.FlashlightPressed`, one new action `Flashlight` bound to `<Keyboard>/f` and `<Gamepad>/leftShoulder` (free,
  reachable without leaving either stick). Ignored while `PlayerController.ControlsLocked`.
- Photometry owned by the component and written to the Light by `ApplyPhotometry()` (called at Awake and by the scene builder):
  spot, outer cone 70 degrees, inner 10 degrees, range 35 m, 4,500 K, soft shadows, unit candela with the reflector option off, and an
  intensity computed so that the stated **300 lm** actually leave the torch: `LumensPerCandela` integrates HDRP's cone falloff
  (squared linear ramp in the cosine, `HDGpuLightsBuilder.Jobs.cs:231-239`, `CommonLighting.hlsl:112-135`) times the beam pattern
  over the cone (0.105 lm per candela) -> **peak 2,860 cd**, a mid-range LED torch.
- Beam pattern generated by code (`BeamPattern(r, angle)`, `CreateCookie(size)`), laid out as HDRP projects a spot cookie (the
  inscribed circle is the outer cone, `LightEvaluation.hlsl:404-431`, `HDGpuLightsBuilder.Jobs.cs:241-245`): Gaussian hot spot
  (radius 0.20 of the cone), a bright ring where it ends, a wide Gaussian spill (0.30 of the peak, width 0.5) fading to nothing at
  the edge, the LED die's slightly darker core, and orange-peel ripples. The scene builder writes it once as
  `ForestData/Lighting/FlashlightBeamPattern.asset` (256 px, grey, mipmapped, clamped; rewritten in place on every build) so edit
  mode, play mode and the player use the same texture; a torch without a cookie generates its own at Awake (`DontSave`).
- Hand-held lag in `LateUpdate`: the light hangs on the camera pivot at the hand (0.22, -0.30, 0.25 m: right, below, in front of the
  eye), aimed at the view's centre 12 m ahead; its world rotation follows `pivot.rotation * rest` with an exponential time constant of
  0.07 s, never more than 10 degrees behind (`SmoothTowards`, frame-rate independent); a jump of the view of more than 1.5 m in one
  frame (teleport, hiding spot, camera cut) snaps instead of swinging.

HDRP side, `P/Assets/Game/Editor/Forest/ForestFlashlight.cs` (new): shadow resolution override 1,024 px, update EveryFrame (the
light moves with the view; a cached map would trail it), near plane 0.1 m, normal / slope bias 0.75 / 0.5, LED radius 0.015 m
(`Light.shapeRadius`; the pipeline's punctual filtering is Medium, PCF, so the shadows are soft by filter), contact shadows off,
affects volumetrics with dimmer **0.04**. `ForestWorldRig.BuildPlayer` calls `ForestFlashlight.Build(pivot)`; the greybox is not
touched.

## 3. The night (step 2): how it was tuned

The night sky was fetched first (`logs/N1/fetch-night-sky.log`: `qwantani_night_puresky`, 2 files, 26.4 MB, md5 ok; it imports
as a BC6H cubemap, 2,048 px faces; calibration 2.03 lux in image units, colour (0.50, 0.49, 0.50)). Tuning used a temporary editor
script (`B/tools/n1/ForestN1TuneTemp.cs`, copied into the project for the tuning runs only and removed afterwards) that renders
views of the built scene under variants of the preset and the torch, changed in memory and restored between variants, with the
torch copied onto the capture camera as the captures do; grey cards (albedo 0.25) at 5 to 30 m along the view measured the beam's
reach, and physics rays the distances in each view. Runs t1 to t14, `B/work/N1/tune/t*` (images, `tune.json`), logs
`B/logs/N1/tune*.log`. Every sheet a decision below rests on was looked at.

| Round | Question | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| t1 | The spec's starting values (auto -1.5 .. 6, mist 25 m, anisotropy 0.2, torch volumetric dimmer 1) | With the torch the frame is a solid white cone: the light sits inside the mist at the hip and the in-scattered light grows as 1/d^2 toward it. Without it, silhouettes against the moonlit mist; the trail and floor invisible |
| t2 | Torch volumetric dimmer 0.03 to 0.25, anisotropy 0.2 / 0.6; torchless exposure | 0.04 to 0.1 leaves a shaft from the hand and a haze ahead and the lit ground readable through it. Forward scattering 0.6 (the day's) hides the near cone better. Torchless -3 EV readable, -1.5 too dark |
| t3, t4 | Beam pattern, cone, lumens, mist 30 m, exposure cap | The flat spill made a hard-edged disc; outer 70 / inner 10 and a Gaussian spill soften it. 10 EV cap so a fir at arm's length is not held back |
| t5 | Reach, cards at 5 to 30 m on trail_start, long_sightline, trail_mid_fwd | A 0.25-albedo card in the hot spot reads 0.25 to 0.47 at 20 to 30 m (torch off: 0.03 to 0.08) with that round's 400 lm variant (final settings: section 5); the mist takes it beyond 30 m. Mist 40 m adds little; 30 m kept |
| t6 to t9 | Clipping of near foliage (crouch_hide, fir_close) | Centre-weighted metering exposed for the dark frame and clipped the fir's twigs (0.7 % of crouch_hide); custom and neutral tonemapping clipped more (1.8 to 2.6 %); a procedural metering disc in the middle of the screen exposes for what the beam lights (0.2 to 0.5 %) |
| t10 to t12 | Full resolution, every close view | Exposed for the beam, ACES's toe crushed 57 to 82 % of eight close views below 2/255 outside the beam, which the capture's black-frame check rejects. A lift turns the blacks grey (rejected); a gamma of +0.2 to +0.25 on the dark tones keeps the forest around the beam faintly visible; 300 lm instead of 400 |
| t13, t14 | Torchless look with the gamma; margin at rock_close | -1.2 EV with gamma +0.25: torchless means 0.026 to 0.080, crushed at most 11 %; rock_close with the torch 38 % crushed (49.6 % at +0.2) |

## 4. Performance (step 3): the player, torch off and on, against D2

Method as D2 (`issues/D2-density.md` section 2): macOS development player built by `B/tools/n1/build-player.sh` (D2's script with
N1 paths; it puts back the post-D1 `HDRP.asset` and the HDRPDefaultResources pair after every build: "settings: unchanged" each
time), 2,560 x 1,440 full-screen window, vsync off, judged by the 16-frame-window median and p95 at the worst view. Every run of
this session was bursty except single views (frames handed back in clumps; D2 section 2), so per-frame p95 is not meaningful;
where a view ran steady its per-frame figures are given. The harness gained (`P/Assets/Game/Diagnostics/ForestFrameHarness.cs`):
`-forestMeasureFlashlight on|off` (the player's torch rides on the measurement camera at the hand's pose, or stays dark with the
rig), `-forestMeasurePreset`, `-forestMeasureTorchShadowRes`, `-forestMeasureTorchShadows off`, `-forestMeasureTorchRange`,
`-forestMeasureTorchVolumetric off`, and `-forestMeasureTorchAB`, a paired mode that records every view three times in a row
(torch off, on, on without shadows), which takes the drift between runs out of the comparison.

The worst eight views, torch off / on / on without shadows recorded back to back within each run, three runs each:

The shadow map's size does not matter: 512 px costs what 1,024 px costs (shadows +0.54 to +0.87 against +0.46 to +0.91 ms); the

by HDRP's range windowing (to 0.35 of its value at 20 m, against 0.80 at 35 m), and the unpaired A/B (`out/N1/ab1-verdict.txt`,
three interleaved rounds of torch off / on / 512 px / no shadows / no volumetric / range 25 / 512 + range 25, eight views) showed
all variants within 0.4 ms of each other, inside the run-to-run noise. The mist (volumetric) part of the torch is below that noise.
The shadow map is updated every frame because the light moves with the view; an on-demand or cached map would trail it.

## 7. Not verified, weak, open

- **Thin frame-time margin with the torch.** The worst view (pano_90) runs 11.3 to 12.5 ms with the torch on, by session; in a slow
  session (pr512, 13:52) it touched 12.48 ms, and its 16-frame p95 reached 14.95 ms. The torch costs about 1 ms, two thirds of it
  its shadow casters. The next lever, not built: the moon's shadows at night (50 m, three cascades of the dense stand, the same cost as
  by day) could be shortened (the mist at 30 m and the moon's 0.3 lux leave little of them visible past 30 m), or the drawer's
  small-mesh culling for shadow views (`gpuResidentDrawerSettings.shadowSmallMeshScreenPercentages`, an `HDRP.asset` change; its
  effect on a spot light's map was not looked into) might keep ferns and branches out of the torch's map. Dinosaurs and their AI will need room too.
- **Per-frame p95** of the player: not meaningful (every run bursty, as in D2); judged by 16-frame figures.
- **ground_close at night**: its camera at 0.4 m puts the hand-held torch a hand's breadth above the litter, so only a strip is lit
  (mean 0.045). Not a pose the player can take; left as it renders, and not used by the night exposure test.
- **The capture's black-frame limit at night**: close views lit by the torch leave much of the frame dark; with the shadow gamma
  rock_close is the closest to the limit, 41.3 % crushed in the final capture against 50 %. A darker grade or a stronger torch would
  push it over; the check was not changed.
- **Clipping near**: at most 0.19 % of a frame (trunk_close, a trunk at 1 m) in the final captures, 0.03 % in the PlayMode test;
  the brightest twigs of a fir at arm's length can still reach white.
- **The lag still** (`trail_start__turning.png`): the log reads the beam's axis 11.2 degrees from the view's while turning at 180
  degrees a second; that is the 10 degree lag limit plus the rest aim, which is 1.8 degrees off the view's axis (the hand converges on
  the view's centre 12 m ahead).
- **Exposure adaptation in play**: switching the torch off leaves the frame dark for about 2 to 3 s while the exposure falls from
  the beam's level to the night's (HDRP's default 1 EV a second); switching on overexposes for a fraction of a second. Intended
  (night vision lost to the torch), not tuned, and it is not tested (the tests cut the camera, which resets exposure).
- **Torch in daylight**: the torch is on in OvercastMist too and adds light near the player; not judged (OvercastMist captures and
  the overcast exposure test run without it).
- **Volumetric beam quality**: medium fog quality; the shaft from the hand is a froxel effect and can show stepping when the light
  moves fast. Not judged in motion beyond the one turning still.
- **No battery**, no sound, no AI reading `IsOn` yet (the event exists).
- `Logs/forest-work/` not updated (as in D1 and D2).
